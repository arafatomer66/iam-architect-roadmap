The premise

An aspiring IAM professional asks how to become an architect.

The usual answer is a product name. It's the wrong answer — and it's why so many people spend two years on certifications and still get told "you're an engineer, not an architect."

✕ The answer that doesn't work

"Learn SailPoint." "Get certified in Okta." "Do the Entra exam."

  • Teaches a console, not a model
  • Doesn't transfer when the product changes
  • Bends every problem toward one tool
  • Can't answer why the organisation grants access at all

✓ What actually makes an architect

Identity isn't a technology problem. It's understanding how businesses work.

  • Who is authoritative for this data — and who wins on conflict
  • Why a permission exists, and when it must end
  • What happens on grant, on revoke, and on the day nobody revoked
  • How to defend the design to auditors, engineers and a CFO

What survives

The tools will change. The principles won't.

ForgeRock became part of Ping. Azure AD became Entra ID. The platform you'll deploy in 2030 doesn't have a name yet. These five statements will still be true.

01

Three different problems

Authentication proves who. Authorisation decides what. Governance proves it was right. Different lifecycles, different owners, different products.

02

Authority or chaos

Identity data has an authoritative source, or it has chaos. Nothing downstream matters if you can't answer "where did this attribute come from?"

03

Revocation is the architecture

Access must have an expiry. Granting is easy and everyone celebrates it. Taking it back reliably, at scale, is the actual engineering.

04

Credentials are liabilities

Every credential is a liability with a benefit attached. Count them, own them, rotate them, kill them — humans and machines alike.

05

UX is a security control

A control people route around protects nothing. The shadow process users invent to escape your design is now your real architecture.

The professionals who succeed won't be the ones who know the most tools.
They'll be the ones who understand identity as the foundation of business security.
Products are how you implement the answer. They are never the answer.
