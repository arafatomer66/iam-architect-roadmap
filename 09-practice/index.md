# Stage 9 — Practice

## Reading produces a comfortable illusion of competence

You can read all of Stages 1–8 and still freeze when someone hands you a marker and says *"design us an identity architecture for this."* Recognition is not recall, and recall is not design.

{: .concept }
> **Architecture is a performance skill.** Like a language, it degrades without use and cannot be learned entirely from books. The exercises here exist to move knowledge from "I've read about that" to "I can produce that under pressure, out loud, while someone challenges me." Do them badly first — that's the point. The gap between your answer and a good one is the actual curriculum.

## How to use this stage

**Attempt before reading.** For every case study, spend fifteen minutes on your own answer first. Reading a worked solution without attempting it teaches you to recognise good architecture, not to produce it.

**Say it out loud.** Interviews and design reviews are verbal. Explaining a trust boundary in speech is a different skill from writing about it, and the first time you attempt it should not be in front of a hiring panel.

**Write down your reasoning, then revisit it.** Keep your answers. Re-read them after finishing the repo. The changes you'd make are the measure of what you learned — and noticing your own changed judgement is how you know it's real.

**Get challenged.** Find someone to poke holes. An unchallenged design feels much better than it is, and the specific experience of defending a decision under scrutiny is the one this stage is trying to simulate.
